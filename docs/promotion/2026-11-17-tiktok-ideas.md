## 1. The meme became homework — recommended

Format: a 15–20 second screen recording with a dry voiceover and large captions.

- Opening: “I started fact-checking the ‘before GTA 6’ meme.”
- Show the Google AI pizza card: “Google's AI suggested glue on pizza.”
- Show the penguin card: “America put tariffs on islands full of penguins.”
- Show the Katy Perry card: “Katy Perry went to space.”
- Scroll through the timeline: “Anyway, I accidentally made a history website.”
- End with beforegta6.wtf visible for two seconds.

Caption: “the side quests got out of hand”

The website is part of the punchline. Existing cards supply the pictures and sources.

## 2. Explaining the future to someone from 2013

Format: a two-character skit, filmed with two camera positions or made with alternating text cards.

- 2013: “Is GTA 6 out yet?”
- Present day: “Katy Perry went to space.”
- 2013: “That wasn't my question.”
- Present day: “The penguins got tariffs.”
- 2013: “What happened to the timeline?”
- Show the website: “I made one. It doesn't help.”

Caption: “trying to explain the patch notes”

The dialogue supplies the joke. Filming and acting require more work than a screen recording.

## 3. The receipt

Format: screen recording, scroll acceleration, a final still frame, and optional voiceover.

- Opening: “I made a SHORT list of things we got before GTA 6.”
- Show three readable cards before accelerating through the timeline.
- Stop briefly at an absurd card, then continue scrolling.
- Ending: “This was supposed to be a joke. Now it has sources.”
- End with beforegta6.wtf visible.

Caption: “the receipt is longer than my attention span”

This can be made without filming a person. The scroll requires readable pauses to preserve the joke.
